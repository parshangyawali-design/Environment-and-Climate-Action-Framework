WHAT CAN BE SOLVED DIGITALLY?

Some problems are information, coordination or access problems.

Software can directly improve how people find, understand, share and act on information. It cannot repair a damaged place by itself.

The boundary: An app can locate a flood report, but people still need to clear drainage safely. A map can flag heat exposure, but communities still need shade, water and accessible cooling spaces.

01
DIGITAL SOLUTIONS

Use data, software and communication where they help

  • ↗
    Monitor and warn

    Share validated heat, smoke and flood alerts from qualified local agencies.

  • ↗
    Map risks and resources

    Combine satellite observations, public data and accessible community maps.

  • ↗
    Measure emissions

    Compare transparent inventories over time, with reporting years and boundaries stated.

  • ↗
    Organize and advocate

    Coordinate volunteers, share verified evidence, submit public comments and track commitments.

  • ↗
    Reduce avoidable digital demand

    Keep devices useful longer, repair them where practical and favour efficient digital services. Cleaner electricity matters to data-centre emissions.

02
IN-PERSON SOLUTIONS

For places, living systems and equipment needing care

  • ↗
    Restore nature with local leadership

    Protect habitats and wetlands with land rights, community leadership and ecological aftercare.

  • ↗
    Make communities safer from heat

    Fund shade, drinking water, accessible cooling spaces and outreach to people at higher risk.

  • ↗
    Prepare for hazards

    Maintain drainage, accessible shelters, evacuation plans and local emergency readiness.

  • ↗
    Repair cooling equipment safely

    Qualified technicians can recover legacy CFCs, fix leaks and safely retire equipment.

  • ↗
    Make change durable

    Combine skilled work, reliable budgets and follow-up inspection instead of one-off campaigns.

Safety first. Never enter floodwater or wildfire areas, handle refrigerants or unknown hazardous materials, or attempt unsafe electrical repairs. Use appropriately trained professionals and follow local emergency services.

WHY TWO TRACKS?

Information helps.
Implementation lasts.

Satellite maps can flag change; ecological expertise, lawful access and community consent are needed before restoration. Flood maps can guide plans; shelters and evacuation routes must work in real conditions.

Pair digital coordination with funded, physically delivered solutions. International agreements demonstrate how policy, trained technicians and safe handling can work together.
