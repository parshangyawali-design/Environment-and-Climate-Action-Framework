03 / The challenge

A changing climate
changes everything.

The climate has always varied. What is different now is the speed and cause: human activities—principally burning fossil fuels, land-use change and agriculture—are warming the atmosphere, ocean and land. [1]

Climate impacts are already affecting people and nature. The risks are unequal: those who contributed least to the problem often have fewer resources to respond.

04 / Match the solution to the problem

Digital tools.
Real-world change.

Digital tools help us measure, coordinate and communicate. Physical problems often also need skilled people, safe equipment, materials, funding and long-term care.

01
SOLVE DIGITALLY

When information and coordination can move the needle

  • ↗
    Monitor and alert

    Share verified heat, wildfire-smoke and flood warnings from official sources.

  • ↗
    Map and prioritize

    Use open maps and satellite observations to guide inspections and protection plans.

  • ↗
    Coordinate and advocate

    Connect volunteers, publish transparent evidence and ask decision-makers for measurable action.

  • ↗
    Cut digital waste

    Extend device life, reduce unnecessary storage and video use, and choose efficient settings; cleaner electricity matters most to digital infrastructure emissions.

02
ACT PHYSICALLY

When a place, material or living system needs care

  • ↗
    Restore with local expertise

    Fund locally led habitat and wetland recovery with ecological monitoring and long-term stewardship.

  • ↗
    Make places safer

    Plant and maintain appropriate urban shade, improve drainage and cooling access, and protect safe evacuation routes.

  • ↗
    Repair and replace equipment

    Use qualified technicians to find refrigerant leaks, recover legacy CFCs and safely dispose of old cooling equipment.

  • ↗
    Organize for lasting change

    Work with residents, scientists and public services on funded, accountable plans—not one-off clean-ups alone.

Safety note Avoid entering floodwater, handling unknown chemicals or refrigerants, disturbing suspected hazardous materials, or attempting electrical or refrigerant repairs. Contact trained local professionals and emergency services.

04 / Evidence matters

Facts worth
holding onto.

The 1.5°C figure is a long-term global limit, not a line that one unusually hot year can cross on its own. In 2024, the World Meteorological Organization estimated annual global temperature at 1.55 ± 0.13°C above the 1850–1900 average—the first calendar year likely above 1.5°C. Long-term warming averaged across decades remains below 1.5°C. [2]

That distinction is not a reason to delay. Every increment of warming increases climate risks, and rapid emissions cuts alongside adaptation can reduce future harm. [1]
